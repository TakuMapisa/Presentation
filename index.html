The basics

What is Cybersecurity?

The practice of protecting systems, networks, data, and devices from digital and physical attacks.

Purpose

  • Safeguard sensitive information
  • Ensure business continuity
  • Maintain trust and compliance
03 / 16
The basics

Key Goals of Cyber Security

🔒 Confidentiality

Protect sensitive information from unauthorized access.

✅ Integrity

Ensure data remains accurate and consistent throughout its lifecycle — free from unauthorized modification.

⚡ Availability

Keep systems and data accessible when needed, resilient to denial-of-service (DoS) attacks.

Threat landscape

Cyber Crimes

Illegal activities carried out using computers or networks.

Examples

  • Identity theft
  • Financial fraud
  • Data breaches
  • Cyber espionage

Impact

Loss of money, data, and trust.

06 / 16
Types of cyber attacks

Phishing & Spear-Phishing

🎣 Phishing

Criminals send fake emails, SMS messages, or links pretending to be a real company or person, tricking you into clicking, sharing information, or downloading something harmful.

🎯 Spear-Phishing & Smishing

A more targeted version of phishing — the attacker personalizes the message using information about you specifically.

07 / 16
Types of cyber attacks

Social Engineering & Malware

🧠 Social Engineering

Criminals manipulate or trick people into giving away information or doing something unsafe — using psychology instead of hacking computers.

🦠 Malware & Ransomware

Malware is harmful software that sneaks in when you click bad links or download unsafe files. Ransomware locks your files and demands payment to unlock them.

08 / 16
Types of cyber attacks (cont.)

Fake Websites & Social Scams

🌐 Fake Websites & Password Theft

Attackers create fake websites that look real (e.g. fake social media login pages). When you enter your username and password, they steal it.

📱 Social Media & Mobile Money Scams

Scams that happen on Facebook, WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram, or through mobile money platforms like EcoCash or OneMoney.

Stay alert

Red Flags to Detect Threats

⚠️ Urgent or threatening language

Pressure to act immediately is a classic manipulation tactic.

✉️ Unexpected attachments or links

Unsolicited files or links from unknown senders.

🔤 Spelling & grammar errors

Official communications are rarely sloppy.

❓ Requests for sensitive info

Legitimate organizations rarely ask for passwords via email.

Defense

How We Can Protect Ourselves

🔑 Turn On Two-Factor Authentication

Even if someone steals your password, they can't get in without the second code.

🖱️ Avoid Unknown Apps or Downloads

Only install apps from official stores. Don't open suspicious attachments.

🪪 Stay Alert on Social Media

Scammers create fake profiles or pretend to be friends. If it feels off — don't trust it.

🔐 Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Avoid simple passwords. Use different passwords for different accounts.

🏦 Check Websites Before Entering Passwords

Look for correct spelling, "https://", and avoid strange or unfamiliar URLs.

11 / 16
Passwords

Why Password Security Matters

Passwords are the #1 target in cyberattacks — over 80% of breaches involve compromised credentials. Weak or reused passwords expose company data, customer information, and systems.

Good Password Practices

  • Use strong, unique, long passwords — e.g. three random words like "DeskAppleDoor" strengthened to "D3skApp!eDOor3"
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
  • Use a password manager
  • Avoid names, birthdays, or reusing passwords
12 / 16
Avoid these habits

What NOT to Do (Bad Password Practices)

  • Using weak passwords (name, date of birth, department, "12345", etc.)
  • Writing passwords on calendars, notebooks, or sticky notes
  • Sharing passwords with colleagues
  • Reusing passwords across multiple accounts
  • Leaving phones, laptops, and tablets without lock passwords

Physical security

Leaving the Machine On and Unattended

Risks of leaving your device on and unattended:

🔓 Unauthorized Access

Someone can physically access the machine and retrieve or modify data.

📂 Data Leakage

If files or systems are open, sensitive information can be copied or viewed.

🦠 Installation of Malware

An attacker can plug in a USB or access the internet to install malicious software.

🔑 Bypassing Authentication

If the machine is not locked, attackers can bypass login controls entirely.

⚡ Resource Drain

Leaves systems vulnerable to remote exploits and consumes power/resources unnecessarily.

14 / 16
Physical security

Best Practices

When leaving your machine, always follow these practices:

🖥️ Lock Your Screen

Press Win + L when stepping away. Enable auto-lock after a period of inactivity.

⏻ Log Out or Shut Down

Always log out or shut down your system when it is not in use.

🔌 Restrict USB & Physical Ports

Block or restrict USB access and physical ports to prevent unauthorised device connections.

🚫 Never Leave It Unlocked

An unattended, unlocked machine is a risk — physically and digitally. Always lock or shut it down.
